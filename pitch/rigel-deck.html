The Assistant — our moat

Autonomy a platform team will
actually turn on.

Anyone can wire an LLM to kubectl. The defensible part is making autonomous remediation safe enough to trust in production. That trust layer is the product.

Three autonomy modes
  • Auto — apply safe-tier fixes automatically
  • Advisory — queue everything for approval
  • Quiet-hours — auto by day, queue overnight
Guardrails, always on
  • An RBAC cage that can't touch Secrets — destructive actions blocked at the cluster
  • Circuit breakers, spend caps, and an instant kill-switch
  • Backup-before-mutate, one-click revert, fail-closed on any error

No API key. The agent runs on your existing Claude subscription — no metered inference bill, no new vendor to procure.

Why now

Two curves just crossed.

Kubernetes became the default
Production K8s is now the norm, not the frontier — 82% of container users run it in prod, 5.6M+ developers worldwide. The operational pain is universal and permanent.
Agents got good enough to act
Frontier models can now reliably investigate and reason over live infrastructure with tools. "Diagnose and fix" is a credible autonomous task — not a demo.

The missing piece was never intelligence — it was trust. A team only hands over the keys if autonomy comes with hard, auditable limits. Rigel is built limits-first. That's why now, and why us.
